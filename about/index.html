How the school began

Emmanuel started with a prayer meeting in 2001. A group of Leicester parents wanted their children educated somewhere Christian faith was not something added on at the end of the day, and they had no idea whether it was possible.

They had no building, no funding and no guarantee it would work. It took two more years of praying and giving before the school opened its doors in August 2003. Two decades on it is still recognisably the same thing: parent-founded, not part of a chain, and dependent on people who believe in it.

The school has never moved. From the first day it has met in Braunstone Evangelical Free Church on Didsbury Street, and that partnership has lasted the whole of its life. Our pupils are taught in a building that belongs to a congregation at the weekend and to children during the week — which is a fair picture of how the school itself works.

From a handful of pupils it has grown to seven classes taking children from Reception through to Year 11, with capacity for seventy-five.

The building

During term time and through the school day, the whole site is ours. We have sole use of it. The church uses the hall and kitchen in the evenings for its own meetings and services, once the children have gone home.

Most classes are taught in our own classrooms in the portakabins on the site. These belong to the school alone and are set up as classrooms and left as classrooms — nothing is packed away each afternoon. Two further classrooms are in the main building.

The hall and kitchen we share with the congregation across the week, but during school hours they function as any school hall and kitchen does.

Our vision

Growing in Christ, Thriving in Learning, Shaping the Future.

Three things in a deliberate order.

Growing in Christ

We want children to know Christ, not merely to know about him. We cannot manufacture that and we do not pretend to. What we can do is teach the Bible faithfully, pray honestly, and let children see adults who are still growing themselves.

Thriving in Learning

A Christian school that teaches badly has failed at being Christian. Excellent teaching is not in tension with faith; it is an expression of taking God’s world seriously. We expect hard work, and we expect pupils to leave able to do things they could not do when they arrived.

Shaping the Future

Children do not stay children. We are preparing people who will work, vote, raise families and make decisions in a world that will not share all their convictions. They need to be able to hold their beliefs thoughtfully and live alongside people who disagree with them.

The school today

  • Pupils aged four to sixteen, taught in seven mixed-age classes.
  • Capacity for seventy-five pupils. We are small on purpose and intend to stay small.
  • Thirteen members of staff, most of whom teach across several classes.
  • An independent school, inspected by the Independent Schools Inspectorate.
  • Part of the Leicestershire Christian Education Trust, a registered charity.
  • Members of the Independent Schools Association and the Association of Christian Schools International.

Who comes here

Most of our families are Christian, but not all of them are, and a family does not have to share our faith to belong here. What we ask is that you are content for your child to be taught within a Christian framework, because it runs through everything and cannot be opted out of.

If you are weighing that up, come and see it. It is much easier to judge in a classroom than on a website.
