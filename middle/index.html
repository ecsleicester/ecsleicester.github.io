Middle School

One teacher becomes several

Oak takes Years 5 and 6, Maple Years 7 and 8. This is where the change happens. A pupil moves from one teacher who knows them in every subject to several teachers who each know them in one. We do it gradually, and the class keeps a form teacher who holds the whole picture.

That form teacher is the person a parent talks to first, and the person who notices when a pupil is fine in three subjects and struggling in a fourth.

Subjects

Pupils are taught English, mathematics, science, history, geography, religious education, modern foreign languages, art, design and technology, music, drama, computing, physical education and PSHE.

World religions

From Year 7 pupils study the major world religions. We teach them as their adherents would recognise them. A pupil should be able to explain what a Muslim or a Hindu believes, and why they find it convincing, before offering any view of their own.

Preparing for GCSE

Middle School is where the habits are built: revising properly, writing at length, working when nobody is checking. Pupils who arrive in Birch without those habits find GCSE much harder than they need to.

Pastoral care

Eleven to thirteen is a hard age anywhere. In a school this size a pupil having a difficult term is noticed by several adults who know them, and a difficult term does not become a reputation.
