## The CHRIST values {#christ}

<p class="lead">Six words that spell out what we are aiming at. They are not decoration on a wall. Staff are appraised against them, pupils are recognised for them, and they are the language we use when something has gone wrong.</p>

<tbody>
<tr><td class="num">Christ-Centred</td><td>Christ is the reason the school exists, not a feature of it. He shapes the curriculum, the discipline and the staffroom.</td></tr>
<tr><td class="num">Hope-Filled</td><td>We believe children can change, including the ones who are finding it hardest. Nobody here is written off at eleven.</td></tr>

## How this shapes behaviour {#behaviour}

Our approach to behaviour is developmental rather than punitive. A child who has behaved badly has something to learn, and the adult’s job is to teach it. That does not mean there are no consequences. It means the consequence is aimed at the future rather than the past.

Because we are small, a pupil cannot become anonymous, and a difficult patch does not become a reputation that follows them for five years.

## The Emmanuel Legacy Programme {#legacy}

Pupils in the secondary years work through a character and achievement framework built around the six values, with awards at four levels: Foundation, Growth, Flourishing and Legacy.

It recognises the things that do not appear on a certificate — service, perseverance, integrity under pressure — and it gives a pupil something to point at when they leave.

## Our aims {#aims}

By the time a pupil leaves us at sixteen, we want them to:

- Know the Bible well enough to read it for themselves and think with it.
- Have qualifications that open the doors they want to walk through.
- Be able to work hard at something difficult without giving up.
- Treat people who disagree with them with genuine respect.
- Know that they were known here, by name, by adults who wanted the best for them.
