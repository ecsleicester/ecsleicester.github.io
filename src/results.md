## Reading our results {#reading}

Our GCSE cohorts are small — usually fewer than a dozen pupils, and in one recent year a single pupil. That has consequences worth understanding before you look at any figures.

One pupil can shift a percentage by ten points or more. A child having a difficult year, or a child doing unexpectedly well, changes the headline in a way it never would at a school of a thousand.

Comparing us year on year, or against a large secondary, therefore tells you very little. We publish the number of pupils alongside every figure so that you can see exactly what each percentage rests on.

We would rather you asked us about these numbers than drew conclusions from them.

## What figures do not show {#progress}

A grade tells you where a pupil finished. It does not tell you where they started, and for a number of our pupils that distance is the real achievement.

Some arrive with us having found school difficult elsewhere. Some arrive partway through their secondary years with ground to make up. Several of the pupils in the years above joined us after a hard time somewhere else, and left with qualifications nobody would have predicted for them.

If you want to understand what we add rather than what we select, ask us about individual pupils when you visit. We will tell you honestly.
