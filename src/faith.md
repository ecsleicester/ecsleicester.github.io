## Our statement of faith {#statement}

<p class="lead">Emmanuel Christian School is an evangelical Christian school. Every member of staff subscribes to this statement, and it shapes what is taught and how the school is run.</p>

## What this means day to day {#practice}

A statement of faith on a website tells you very little on its own. What it looks like in a school is more useful.

- The Bible is taught as true, and taught properly, with attention to what it actually says.
- We hold to the biblical account of creation, and pupils are taught it. They are also taught the scientific theories they will meet at GCSE and beyond, accurately and in full, because a pupil who cannot give a fair account of a view they do not hold has not been educated in it.
- Worship happens daily. It is not performance, and children are not pressured into professions of faith.
- Article 5 is the one that shapes the ordinary school day most. Every child in this building carries inherent and equal dignity because of how they were made, not because of how they behave, how quickly they learn, or how easy they are to teach. That is why a child who has got something badly wrong is still treated with respect while it is put right.

## Other faiths, and none {#others}

We admit children from families who do not share our beliefs, and they are treated no differently once they are here. Nobody is required to profess faith, and nobody is thought less of for not having it.

From Year 7 pupils study the major world religions. We teach them as their adherents would recognise them, not as caricatures to be knocked down. Pupils should be able to explain what a Muslim, a Hindu or an atheist believes and why they find it convincing. Understanding a view properly is a precondition of disagreeing with it honestly.

If you are considering the school and are not a Christian family, come and talk to us. It is a conversation we have often and are glad to have.

## Faith and life in modern Britain {#values}

We actively promote the values of democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, and mutual respect and tolerance of those with different faiths and beliefs. This is not a box we tick alongside our Christian convictions. It follows directly from what we believe about people: if every person carries the image of God, then every person is owed respect, including those who disagree with us profoundly.

Pupils here will meet views that conflict with what they are taught at home and at school. We would rather they met them in a classroom, explained fairly by a teacher who knows them, than for the first time at eighteen with nobody to talk it through with.

## Staff and the statement {#staff}

Teaching and support staff are appointed on the basis of their agreement with the statement of faith as well as their professional competence. Where a role carries an occupational requirement of this kind, it is stated in the advertisement.
