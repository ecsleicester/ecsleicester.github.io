Who we admit

We admit children from the age of four through to sixteen. Most join us at the beginning, in Willow, but children arrive at every stage and we are used to settling them.

You do not have to be a Christian family. Many of our families are, and some are not. What we ask is that you are content for your child to be taught within a Christian framework, because it runs through the whole school and is not something a pupil can be excused from.

We are not a specialist setting. If your child has significant additional needs, talk to us early and we will tell you honestly whether we can meet them well. That conversation is much better had before an application than after one.

Start with a visit

We would rather you came on an ordinary Tuesday than to a polished open evening. You will see classes actually teaching, which tells you far more.

  • Visits are with the Head Teacher and take about an hour.
  • They happen during the school day, in term time.
  • Bring your child. We would like to meet them, and they will have opinions.
  • Ask us anything. Including the awkward questions.

Book a visit

Tell us your child’s age, when you are hoping they would start, and which days usually suit you. We will be in touch to arrange a time.

How to apply

The process, start to finish
 StepWhat happens
1Get in touchFill in the visit form above, or ring the school. Tell us your child’s age and when you are hoping they would start.
2Visit the schoolAn hour with the Head Teacher, during a normal working day.
3ApplyWe give you the application form after the visit. confirm whether there is an application fee
4Records and conversationWe ask your child’s current school or nursery for information, with your permission, so that we start from where they actually are.
5Taster daysYour child spends time in the class they would join. confirm how many days
6OfferA written offer of a place, subject to the admissions policy and to accepting the terms of admission.

Places and waiting lists

The school has capacity for seventy-five pupils across seven classes. Because classes are small, individual classes fill before the school does, and a place may be available in one year group and not another.

Where a class is full we keep a waiting list. Confirm how the waiting list is ordered and how long a name stays on it

Joining part way through

Children arrive mid-year for all sorts of reasons, including because school has not been going well somewhere else. That is not a mark against a child. We will want to understand what has happened, and we will want the previous school’s account as well as yours, but a difficult history does not rule anyone out.
